    How it works

    Source. SEC Form 4 and Form 5 (and their amendments) for every issuer in the security mapping, over the last 21 trading days by New York filing day. "Eligible" shows the frozen eligible universe; "On my lists" the Hot tape, Growth and Cheap lists; "On my watchlist" the stocks you starred, kept in this browser. Not a forecast and not advice.

    Signal. A label from the filings, not a score: Group buy, incl. officer (two or more insiders bought within 10 sessions, at least one an officer such as the CEO or CFO), Group buy, directors, Officer buy, Director buy, Sale, no plan (no 10b5-1 trading plan), Plan or tax sale (under a 10b5-1 plan or to cover tax), Fund/10% owner buy or sale. On the 5%+ Stakes tab: New stake, active (13D), Switched to active (13D), Stake change (13D or 13G), New stake, passive (13G), Below 5%. Checks ●●○ shows the three checks below in order: several insiders within 10 sessions; the CEO or CFO bought; a holding up 5% or more (or new) on $50K or more.

    vs paid / vs sold. Percentage difference between the latest close in the weekly price data and the average price reported by insiders in this window (omitted when a stock split occurred during or after the trades). "Below insider price" filters $50K+ buys trading at least 2% below insiders' average purchase price. Context on current price, not a buy signal.

    Ranking. Buys are sorted by checks met, then amount. Checks counts how many of three facts hold: several insiders bought within 10 sessions; the CEO or CFO bought; a holding rose 5% or more, or is new, on $50K or more. All is sorted by signal type (buy clusters, single buys, sales without a plan, big holders, then planned or routine sales), then newest filing; Sells and Funds & 10% by amount, with sales without a plan first. Click a column name to sort: Signal, Who & context (Checks on Buys), vs paid, Amount or Filed. On phones, use the Sort menu. Search looks at every company: typing starts in All, each tab count shows its matches, and an exact ticker comes first. NEW marks a filing from the last 7 days of data.

    Tested (Leg D, 6 Oct 2026). In eligible stocks, 3,122 insider purchases filed Jan 2023 – Jun 2026 did not beat the average eligible stock over the next 1, 3 or 6 months; groups of insiders and CEO or CFO buys did not either. Use this page as research context — who traded, how much, and whether it is unusual — not as a buy signal. Limits: only companies listed today, one 3.5-year period.

    Short interest. From FINRA's twice-monthly files: shares sold short and days to cover (shares short ÷ average daily volume), from the latest report public by the data date (FINRA publishes 7 business days after each settlement date). "Lightly shorted" or "heavily shorted": the bottom or top 10% of eligible stocks of similar size (market-cap thirds) by days to cover. Tested (Leg D, 6 Oct 2026, eligible stocks, reports Jan 2023 – Sep 2026): six months later the lightly shorted group was about 3.5% ahead of the average eligible stock and the heavily shorted group about 2.4% behind, each a little over two standard errors after allowing for overlapping periods; after one month the gap was small, and short interest as a share of shares outstanding showed nothing. Published studies agree in direction (Hong, Li, Ni, Scheinkman & Yan; Boehmer, Huszar & Jordan). One period, companies listed today only, many numbers tested: context, not a forecast.

    Purpose and plan. From the SEC transaction code, the filing's 10b5-1 box and the footnotes attached to each trade. Codes P and S can be open-market or private; a trade is called private only when its footnote says so. The plan adoption date is shown when a footnote states it.

    Who. Officers and directors are kept apart from 10% owners and funds. A fund's joint filing with its board representative counts as the fund's. Names appear as filed.

    Counted once. Joint filers and amendments that restate their original. A same-day purchase and sale of the same shares at the same price, such as a sale to one's own family trust, is one holding moving, not buying or selling.

    Held back to check. Amendments that change only part of a filing, identical trades by unrelated filers, other share classes, and trades whose price is far from the stock's close or larger than the company.

    Holding change. One disclosed account within one filing, not total wealth; unknown when balances do not reconcile.

    Prices. Close, 3-month move and chart come from the weekly price data (adjusted closes) against QQQ. "Since the first filing" counts only trading days that have passed. No chart when days are missing or the series shows a split mix-up.

    History. Each person's open-market buys and sales (codes P and S) in the SEC Insider Transactions Data Sets, from the filing dates shown in the 3Y History tab, before their first trade in this window; the page itself still covers one month. "No buy …" means none in those filings. Routine or opportunistic follows Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski (2012): someone with a trade in each of the three previous calendar years is routine if they traded in the same calendar month in all three, otherwise opportunistic.

    5%+ ownership reports. Schedule 13D and 13G filed in the window, joined by the company named inside the filing. A 13G is the short form for passive holders (Rule 13d-1(b) institutions, (c) passive investors under 20%, (d) exempt holders); a 13D is the full form for holders who do not claim to be passive, which on its own does not mean an activist campaign. The stake is the largest percent any member of the filing group reports, as of the event date. "Below 5%": the 13G box says the holder no longer owns more than 5%, or an amendment reports under 5%. The purpose text is the holder's own (13D Item 4), shortened to 2,000 characters.

    Colours. Green and red mark rising and falling percentages; on labels, green is buying and amber is selling.
