      How it works

      1. Find. Each Saturday, Python lists the week's SEC 8-K filings for about 4,800 US companies and keeps earnings releases (Item 2.02). This page shows releases from companies in the screener universe: market value at least $1 billion, average daily trading at least $10 million, and a share price of at least $5. Smaller companies are read too and only counted here.
      2. Quote. Python copies passages from the press release word for word: the guidance section, the results, and the guidance from the same company's previous release. A previous passage is left out when it covers a different fiscal period, because comparing next year's guidance with last year's is not a raise.
      3. Read. Jev answers two questions from those passages: what the release establishes about forward guidance, and what the operating evidence shows. A release that also announces a product gets a third question about it.
      4. Check. The floor is Python arithmetic: diluted shares up at most 10% in a year, a positive operating or free-cash-flow margin, and less than a 60% fall from the 1-year high. A missing number fails.

      "Raised" means raised against the company's own previous guidance, not against what analysts expected. A raise below expectations can still send the price down. The 3-day reaction is the stock minus SPY over the three sessions around the first session after the filing.

      Jev is an AI model from TypeSafe. Its answers are generated by AI and can be wrong. Quotes are copied from SEC filings and may be shortened; tables lose their layout in plain text, so open the release for the original. Research, not advice.
