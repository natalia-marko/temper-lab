Does the read help?

The read describes a release. These are the tests of whether it says anything about returns after it.

    How it works
    1. Find. Each Saturday, Python takes the Research Shortlist and each company’s latest quarterly earnings release on or before Friday. The release is checked by its content: an 8-K cover, an interim update or a personnel announcement is not taken as the earnings release.
    2. Quote. Python copies, word for word, the guidance passage (or the results when there is no guidance), the same company’s previous guidance passage, and the sentences that state a change. Passages are shortened; open the filing for the original.
    3. Read. Jev, an AI model from TypeSafe, answers two questions from those passages: what the release says about guidance against the company’s previous guidance, and what the operating evidence shows. Each answer carries Jev’s own confidence. Below 0.50 it is marked “AI unsure”; the number is not a probability of being right.
    4. Check. Python arithmetic, no AI: diluted shares up at most 10% in a year, a positive operating or free-cash-flow margin, and less than a 60% fall from the 1-year high. A missing number is marked “?” and does not pass. The checks never remove a company.

    “Raised” means raised against the company’s own previous guidance, not against what analysts expected. A raise below expectations can still send the price down. When the two guidance passages cover different periods, a raise counts only if the release states it in a sentence.

    The reaction is the stock minus QQQ, in percentage points, over the 3 sessions around the first session after the filing; the later figure covers the 21 sessions after that. Both are realized moves, not a forecast. Next results dates come from Yahoo’s calendar and can move.

    Jev is an AI model from TypeSafe. Its answers are generated by AI and can be wrong. Quotes are copied from the filings and shortened; tables lose their layout in plain text, so open the release for the original. Research, not advice.
